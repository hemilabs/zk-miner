## (b) Recycle policy (LEADS #37)

`crates/zkminer-prover/src/dispatcher.rs`:
- `recycle_after_proofs_for(gpu_tag)`: Metal 200 (`METAL_RECYCLE_AFTER_PROOFS`), everything else 20 as before.
  `ZKMINER_RECYCLE_AFTER_PROOFS` still overrides every slot.
- The memory-growth net (`metal_idle_growth`, pure; `settle_footprint`, the bounded wait; `metal_idle_outgrown`,
  the macOS glue; `metal_net_config`): at the recycle check before each proof it reads the idle worker's
  `phys_footprint` (`memory::macos_pid_footprint`), keyed by slot and pid (a respawn starts a new history),
  and recycles the worker, logged as `warn` with the numbers, when its SETTLED footprint is more than the margin
  over its baseline. This is the fix-round-3 design; rounds 1 and 2 (and why they failed review) are in the fix-round sections at the end of this note.

### Design

Constants (`dispatcher.rs`, each with its measurement): `METAL_IDLE_RECLAIM` 2 s (slowest reclaim 1.30 s,
earlier review trace 1.6 s), `METAL_IDLE_FRESH` 50 ms (earliest first step 90 ms), `METAL_IDLE_SETTLE` 3 s (the
longest one wait may take), poll 250 ms, `METAL_IDLE_AGREE` 64 MiB (settled readings differ by 0-1 MiB),
`METAL_IDLE_TOLERANCE` 256 MiB, `METAL_IDLE_VERIFY_EVERY` 50 (100 before W7 fix round 1),
`METAL_IDLE_ALLOWANCE_CAP_QUARTERS` 3 (the allowance cap, 3/4 of the limit; W7 fix round 1),
`METAL_IDLE_BASELINE_PROOFS` 10.

- **Last use.** Every exchange on a slot records when it ended (`WorkerSlot::last_use`: Prove, Benchmark,
  Warmup, Execute, program id). For a Metal Prove it also records the footprint read right then (one
  `proc_pid_rusage`): a fresh reading with the whole transient.
- **Reading age.** At least 2 s after the last use: settled by definition, compared directly. At most 50 ms
  after a proof: fresh. Anything else: mid-reclaim, an upper bound only. A mid-reclaim reading after a proof is
  judged on the fresh reading taken as that proof ended.
- **Every judgement compares a settled level, or an upper bound of one, with the line**
  (`line = baseline + margin`):
  - settled reading over the line: one more agreeing reading (`Confirm`, 0.25 s), then recycle;
  - any reading at or under the line: passes (the settled level can only be lower);
  - mid-reclaim reading over the line (after a non-proof use): wait until a reading is under the line, or
    settled;
  - fresh reading over the line: judged as reading minus the ALLOWANCE (the transient measured at the last full
    settle of a fresh reading, or for free by the last settled-by-time reading after a proof whose end was
    read). Over the line by that estimate: a full settle, then the verdict on the settled level.
- **The allowance is bounded and kept honest** (as of W7 fix round 1):
  - it is the latest measurement (never above the largest one), capped at 3/4 of the working-set limit
    (the measured 2644 MiB transient is 65 % of a 4 GiB limit), and reset with the worker;
  - its ANCHOR is the fresh reading it was measured on. A fresh reading more than 256 MiB under the anchor
    means the transient may have shrunk (the estimate would come out too low), so it is re-measured by a full
    settle (`Shrank`). This is what stops one huge transient from widening the margin for the worker's life.
    Because the anchor moves only when the allowance is measured, a transient that shrinks a little every
    proof adds up to a re-measure; the round-3 per-proof comparison missed that (review: 250 MiB/proof of
    shrink hid a 50 MiB/proof leak until proof 81);
  - after the baseline proofs, a re-measure that agrees with the allowance (within 64 MiB) keeps the LOWER
    anchor. A healthy worker's settled level is trimmed by 240-400 MiB about every sixth proof and grows
    back (the review's 36-proof 4 GiB trace, below). Compared with the previous fresh reading, every trim
    looked like a shrinking transient and cost a 2.25 s settle (the review's finding: 44 waits per 500
    back-to-back proofs). Against the lowest anchor it costs at most one per worker;
  - a fresh reading under the line that is also under the anchor passes (the settled level is lower still),
    but the next fresh reading over the line is VERIFIED;
  - every 50 fresh readings judged with it, the next one over the line is verified against the line itself:
    a pass/fail settle that stops at the first reading under the line (0.5 s at 8 GiB, the first reclaim
    step; 1.5 s at 4 GiB, where only the second step gets under a 1 GiB margin), and recycles on a settled
    level over it. That bounds what a transient turning into retained memory can hide (nothing in a
    back-to-back reading shows it: the fresh reading stays flat) to 50 proofs' growth, at most one
    allowance;
  - it is never used for a settled or a mid-reclaim reading, and a reading within it is still compared (the
    estimate), never skipped.
- **Baseline from settled readings only**: the maximum settled level of the first 10 proofs. A baseline proof's
  reading is settled when it could raise the baseline by more than 256 MiB (or when no baseline exists yet), so
  ten back-to-back baseline proofs no longer put the transient in it. It can sit up to 256 MiB below the true
  maximum (the simulation checks that bound).
- **Margin** `metal_growth_margin`: `clamp(limit / 4, 1 GiB, 2 GiB)`, and at most half of (M6 ceiling - limit),
  never below 512 MiB. `limit` is the slot's working-set limit (host figure and
  `RISC0_METAL_MAX_WORKING_SET_BYTES`, the slot's spawn environment first); the ceiling is the slot's or miner's
  `ZKMINER_WORKER_MAX_FOOTPRINT_BYTES`, else the RAM - 3 GiB the spawn hands the worker. Why: the worker's
  proof peak is about limit + 2 GiB of host memory (10.73 GB = 10.0 GiB at the 8 GiB tier), a leak adds to it,
  and M6's ceiling kills the worker MID-PROOF once peak + leak passes it. On a 16 GB Mac (8 GiB tier, 13 GiB
  ceiling) that happens at ~3 GiB of leak, so the net must fire well before: 2 GiB. At 4 GiB the healthy
  settled level is ~2.3 GB and the tier's whole job peak 5.5-7.2 GB, so 1 GiB (a quarter of the budget) is a
  large relative growth yet three times the cache growth of the first proofs. Above 8 GiB the absolute 2 GiB cap
  keeps the fixed-size leak signal (it is the same host leak at any limit). Every tier PLAN 9.1 names comes out
  1 GiB (4 GiB budget) or 2 GiB (8 GiB and up); the ceiling term only matters for a ceiling within 4 GiB of the
  limit.
- **Bounded waits, logged.** A wait runs under the slot lock before a proof: at most 3 s, and never more than
  the time left above the start floor (`abort_at - MIN_ABORT_START_BUDGET - VRAM_RECLAIM_WAIT`); without time,
  no wait, no verdict and nothing learned. A wait that cannot reach the reclaim time returns no verdict (an
  unsettled reading never recycles). The deadline is re-checked after it (unchanged). Every wait is logged at
  `info`: why, the reading, its age and the result.

## (b) Proving-thread QoS (LEADS #40)

Probe (`qos/q.c`, `qos/w.c`):
- On macOS a `pthread_create`d thread does **not** inherit its creator's QoS: it starts at default (0x15).
- `taskpolicy -b` sets the Darwin background role. `setpriority(PRIO_DARWIN_PROCESS, 0, 0)` clears it from
  inside: an 8-thread CPU loop took 0.66 s under `-b` against 0.50 s after clearing.
- A clamp set with `taskpolicy -c background|utility` cannot be lifted from inside (0.66 s either way).

`metal_start::apply_proving_qos()`, first thing in `main`:
- clears the background role if set;
- sets the main thread to user-initiated (0x19);
- builds rayon's global pool (risc0's CPU work) with a `start_handler` that does the same per thread. The
  `rayon` dependency is the same 1.11 as risc0's, so this is that pool.

It logs what it found, and warns about a background launch or a low-QoS clamp it cannot lift.

## (b) Power-mode guard (LEADS #58)

## Tried and rejected

- **Prewarming the eval PSO without waiting** (`wait = false`) in `--benchmark`. Rejected: the first program's
  first segments would run the plain kernel with a cold OS cache (+0.27 s on memory-merkle, measured as the
  A arm of `ab/mmcold`), which understates the benchmark. Waiting costs nothing in IPC mode, where it overlaps
  idle time.
- **Running the ctrl prewarm after the eval prewarm** (serially). Rejected without timing: the eval build is
  compile-only and the ctrl commit is GPU-only, so they run side by side (scoped threads).
- **A memory-growth net on the raw idle footprint vs the first proof's reading.** Rejected after the
  `footprint` probes. The reading swings by 2.5 GB with how soon after a wrap it is taken (4.74 GB at 0 s,
  2.09 GB at 5 s), which would recycle healthy workers. The maximum-of-first-10 baseline absorbs that swing
  only when back-to-back readings are among the first 10; fix round 1 adds the settle re-read for the rest.
- **Two consecutive agreeing readings as the only settle criterion** (fix round 3, from the brief). Rejected
  after the 20 ms traces: the reading stands still for up to 0.5 s between the two reclaim steps, so a
  250 ms poll would call a half-reclaimed level settled. Agreement counts only after the 2 s reclaim time.
- **Settling every reading over the line** (fix round 3). Sound, but under back-to-back proofs with a settled
  baseline every reading is over the line (transient 2.64 GB > margin), so every proof would wait ~2.3 s
  (~30 % of a 7 s proof). The fresh-reading estimate with a re-measured, bounded allowance keeps that to
  the baseline waits plus a verification every 50 proofs (W7 fix round 1 numbers in the simulation section).
- W7 fix round 1, all in the simulation with the real wait loop (`r1/`), 500 proofs:
  - **Comparing each fresh reading with the previous one** (round 3): 43 waits, 96.75 s back-to-back on the
    measured trims (one per trim). Replaced by the anchor.
  - **An anchor that every measurement moves, including the free one after an idle gap**: the alternating
    regime on the measured trims paid 20 waits (45 s at 8 GiB), because an idle-gap measurement at a peak
    put the anchor above the next trim. Replaced by the keep-the-lower-anchor rule when re-measures agree.
  - **Dropping the allowance when a fresh reading dips under the line and the anchor** (instead of marking it
    for verification): the two-transient-sizes case at 8 GiB back-to-back waited 255 times for 573.75 s (a
    full settle each time) against 146.75 s with a verification (0.5 s each). Both catch the dip-and-return
    leak at its crossing.
  - **A verification every 25 proofs**: 8 GiB plateau back-to-back 18 verifications, 9.0 s after the baseline,
    against 9 and 4.5 s at 50; 4 GiB would pay 1.5 s each. 50 was kept for cost; what it leaves open is in
    the open issues.
  - **The review's other option, a pass/fail settle for `Shrank`**: not taken. A pass/fail answer does not
    re-measure the allowance, so with the anchor unchanged every later proof would ask again; with the
    anchor rule a healthy worker reaches `Shrank` at most once per life (0-1 in every healthy run).
- **A larger fixed margin (3.5-4 GiB) instead of the re-read** (review option). Not taken: on a 16 GB Mac
  (10.7 GB peak at 8 GiB, 13 GiB self-ceiling) a 4 GiB margin is most of the headroom, so the net would add
  little over M6's ceiling.
- **Raising rayon/main threads to user-interactive.** Not tried. That class is for UI threads, and
  user-initiated already restores full speed under a background launch.

## Knobs

- `ZKMINER_METAL_PREWARM=0|off|false`: no first-job prewarm (pipelines are still built).
- `ZKMINER_WORKER_QOS=0|off|false`: leave scheduling as launched.
- `ZKMINER_METAL_RECYCLE_AFTER_PROOFS=<n>`: the Metal count (20 restores the old behaviour, 0 disables the

## Open issues

1. **risc0 (groth16 owner): a release API for the witness graph.** It is ~1.4-2 GB of host memory held for
   the process life, the largest host item at the 4 and 8 GiB tiers. G13 builds it in ~0.9 s, and the zkvm
   already preloads it at proof start. Dropping it after each wrap on small budgets would cut ~1.5 GB from
   every STARK's footprint at no latency cost (needs `OnceLock` -> a releasable cache).
2. **risc0 (zkp HAL / groth16-sys): freed GPU buffers stay in the footprint for up to ~5 s** (2.47 GB after a
   wrap at 8 GiB) and add ~0.7 GB to the next back-to-back job's peak. Try `setPurgeableState(Empty)` before
   releasing pool and key buffers.
3. **Protocol / host (`benchmark.rs`): carry the power state into the device row.** A `BenchmarkEntry` or
   `CapabilitiesReport` field, so pricing can discount or redo a benchmark taken in Low Power Mode, on
   battery or throttled. The worker logs and prints it today.
4. A recycled worker's first Prove arrives at once, so (a) cannot help it (`ab/ipc0`: no change). A dispatcher
   that respawned a due worker right after a proof, instead of before the next one, would let the prewarm run
   in the idle gap.
5. With a cold OS shader cache, `--benchmark` and an immediate IPC `Benchmark` wait ~6.4 s for the
   specialized eval PSO (19 s with no archive at all). Shipping R20 specializations in the AOT archives (Z16
   open issue) would remove it.
6. Growth net (as of W7 fix round 1):
   - Under back-to-back proofs, a transient that turns into retained memory (the fresh reading stays flat
     while the settled level rises) is invisible until the next verification: up to 50 proofs' growth, at
     most one allowance (~2.6 GB). Simulated: crossings at proofs 16-49 caught at 61-63. A verification every
     25 proofs would halve that window at about twice the verification cost. Any idle gap of 2 s or more
     catches it at once (free re-measure).
   - After the baseline proofs the anchor is the lowest agreeing fresh reading, so a shrinking transient is
     noticed only once the fresh reading falls 256 MiB under that low point. With a leak raising the settled
     level, up to (settled level - its lowest point since the anchor was set) + 256 MiB of transient shrink
     goes unnoticed until the next verification.
   - If the transient changed between job shapes (simulated with 2644 / 1600 MiB alternating), a
     back-to-back worker would wait before most proofs: 255 waits (~145 s) per 500 at 8 GiB, more at 4 GiB.
     It is the wrap's memory and measured the same for two job sizes (fibonacci 100000 and 400000) at both
     budgets; other guests were not traced.
   - The wait cost is from the simulation (the real wait loop on the measured reclaim shape) and the real-leak
     test's baseline waits (5 x 0.78-2.31 s), not from a long healthy back-to-back run through the dispatcher;
