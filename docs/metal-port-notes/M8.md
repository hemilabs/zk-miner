# M8: small-Mac segment-size tiers (adopting risc0 Z12)

Branch `item/M8` of zk-miner (worktree `/Users/user/zkminer/zk-miner-wt-M8`, based on `metal` 789fed5),
built against the fork `deps/risc0` `zkminer/metal` 6234335ab (Z12, G13, G14 merged). risc0 is not edited.
The worktree's `../deps/risc0` path patches resolve to `/Users/user/zkminer/deps/risc0` (checked).

## Summary

Two changes, each with a revert knob:

1. **Tier table one po2 higher below 12 GiB** (`metal_sizing.rs`, shared by host and worker). The host's
   slot sizing (`benchmark::risc0_slot_job_po2`, used by `run.rs::risc0_slot_sizes`), the benchmark
   device row, the Metal benchmark deadline, and the worker's default segment size and hard po2
   ceiling all read it. Knob: `ZKMINER_METAL_PO2_TABLE=m1` restores M1's table and ceiling. The worker
   inherits the miner's environment, so one setting moves both sides.

   | working-set limit | M1 table | M8 table | po2 ceiling, M1 -> M8 | Mac |
   |---|---|---|---|---|
   | < 6 GiB (4 GiB tier) | 17 | **18** | 17 -> 18 | 16 GB under a ~4 GB cap |
   | 6 to < 12 GiB | 18 | **19** | 19 (20 from 10 GiB) | 16 GB (8 GiB figure), 18 GB (10 GiB) |
   | 12 to < 20 GiB | 19 | **20** | 20 | 24 GB (16 GiB) |
   | >= 20 GiB | 20 | 20 | unchanged | 32 GB and up |

   The worker's `metal_host` copies of the table, the peak model and the ceiling now delegate to
   `metal_sizing`, so there is one table. `stark_peak_bytes` (the unconstrained, stacked
   recursion-phase peak) is kept unchanged for the Groth16 keep decision and the device row's memory
   figure. It no longer sizes jobs. The new `min_limit_for_po2` sizes them: the bottom of the budget
   row that proved that size. Above po2 20 it is `stark_peak_bytes`, so the swap-thrash ceiling (21 at
   28 GiB, 22 refused) is unchanged.
2. **The worker drops the segment prover before recursion by default.** At start the worker sets
   `RISC0_METAL_RELEASE_SEG_BEFORE_REC=1` (Z12's forced mode) when the operator set nothing. Z12's
   `auto` mode only sees an *enforced* limit, and a real Mac enforces none. Without this, a 16 GB Mac
   at po2 19 would keep the segment pool beside recursion in real RAM. Knob: set the variable yourself
   (`auto` = Z12 default, `0` = pre-Z12).

**Result** (worker `--benchmark` memory-merkle, quiet machine, load 2.4-5, 7 interleaved pairs per row,
unmodified `metal` build as A; every proof wrapped and verified):

|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

| 8 GiB | 18 | 19 | 16.37 [16.35-16.46] s | 12.04 [12.03-12.05] s | **-26 %** | 8.41 -> 8.73 GB |

| default (28.1 GiB) | 20 | 20 | 9.24 [9.21-9.37] s | 9.20 [9.18-9.27] s | no change | 16.15 -> 14.19 GB |

Full six-program suite, one run each (indicative; same session): at 8 GiB the summed STARK time is

## Measurements

Tools: `scratchpad/w5c/M8/round.sh` runs the worker binary fresh per run, under
`/usr/bin/time -l` (process lifetime peak footprint), with `BENCH_ONLY` and
`RISC0_METAL_MAX_WORKING_SET_BYTES`. All rows are in `rounds.txt`, logs in `runs/`. Binaries: A = `metal`
789fed5; B = table change only; R = B plus forced release; C = final (table + worker release default).
The machine was quiet for this item. A-vs-A spread was at most 0.16 s per row (min-max above), so
every claimed change is 10-80x the spread.

### Feasibility of each new tier (B, table only)

| budget | po2 | STARK | Metal peaks MiB | footprint | result |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 4 GiB | 18 | 17.0 s | 2492 / 3337 / 3942 | 7.53 GB | verified |
| 8 GiB | 19 | 12.1 s | 4784 / 4788 / 5347 | 8.79 GB | verified |
| 10 GiB | 20 | 9.7 s | 9420 / 6896 / 6896 | 13.44 GB | verified |
| 12 GiB | 20 | 9.7 s | 9420 / 7740 / 7740 | 13.67 GB | verified |

po2 20 proves at 10 GiB, but its segment phase is at 92 % of the limit. **Decision: the default
switches to 20 at 12 GiB, and the ceiling admits 20 from 10 GiB.** The reason is an 18 GB Mac, whose
host figure is exactly 10 GiB. It enforces no budget, and a po2 20 proof's process footprint (13.4-13.7
GB) would be about 70 % of its RAM, against 8.4 GB at po2 19. An operator can still ask for 20 there
(Settings po2), and the worker allows it. 10 and 12 GiB were Z12's unmeasured boundaries.

### Sub-track: release the segment prover before recursion (B vs R, 7 interleaved pairs each)

| budget | po2 | B (auto) | R (=1) | footprint B -> R | recursion peak B -> R |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 8 GiB | 19 | 12.11 [12.05-12.18] s | 12.04 [12.01-12.09] s | 8.80 -> 8.72 GB | 4788 -> 3096 MiB |
| 10 GiB | 19 | 11.61 [11.57-11.68] s | 11.55 [11.53-11.56] s | **10.57 -> 8.38 GB** | 6476 -> 4784 MiB |
| 16 GiB | 20 | 9.67 [9.64-9.71] s | 9.65 [9.63-9.70] s | 13.66 -> 13.48 GB | 7740 -> 6044 MiB |
| default | 20 | 9.22 [9.20-9.29] s | 9.22 [9.20-9.24] s | **16.15 -> 14.19 GB** | 11116 -> 9420 MiB |

Time: no change in any row (differences under 0.07 s, inside the spread). This settles Z12's open
question on a quiet machine: its r3 slowdown with `=1` was contention. Memory: up to 2.2 GB less
footprint. The 10 GiB row is closest to a real 16 GB Mac at po2 19: the fork limit there is
`recommendedMaxWorkingSetSize`, about 10.7 GB, and nothing is enforced. With the release, that Mac's
po2 19 memory-merkle footprint (8.38 GB) is no higher than its po2 18 footprint today (8.41 GB). Kept as
the worker default.

### Process footprint over the whole suite (not only memory-merkle)

| budget | A (old tier) | C (M8) |
|---|---|---|
| 4 GiB | - | 7.85 GB (po2 18) |
| 8 GiB | 9.92 GB (po2 18) | 11.30 GB (po2 19) |
| 10 GiB | 9.89 GB (po2 18) | 11.80 GB (po2 19) |

**Peak memory does go up for the 16 GB tier, by 1.4-1.9 GB of lifetime footprint over the full
suite.** The rise comes from the large programs' po2 19 segment phase: at 10 GiB the segment pools
reach 9.0-9.3 GB of Metal allocation, against 6.7-7.1 GB at po2 18. On a 16 GB Mac the footprint would
be about 11.8 GB of 16 GB. That is still under the worker's self-enforced footprint ceiling of 13 GiB
(`ZKMINER_WORKER_MAX_FOOTPRINT_BYTES` = RAM less 3 GiB, M6), so a runaway ends as a bounded slot failure,
not swap. If owner-active use on 16 GB Macs proves this too tight, `ZKMINER_METAL_PO2_TABLE=m1` restores
po2 18. A host-RAM-aware tier is the open issue below. At 4 GiB the footprint goes down: 7.99 to
6.77 GB on memory-merkle.

  full suite. The STARK path itself is unchanged; only the segment size and when the cached prover is

    the default, never refuses 4 GiB, and is unchanged above po2 20.
  - `metal_host`: table, ceiling, release default.
  - `benchmark`: a Mac's slot gets the M8 row (19 / 20 / 20), and M1's is still reachable.

- Builds: the metal and cpu worker bins and `zkminer-cli` (release) pass.

## Tried and rejected

- Po2 20 from 10 GiB as the default (Z12's lower bound). It proves, but it is rejected for the 18 GB
  Mac's host RAM (above). It is kept as the ceiling only.
- Making the release threshold depend on the limit. It is not needed, because `=1` measured
  time-neutral at every budget including the default.

## Knobs

- `ZKMINER_METAL_PO2_TABLE=m1`: M1's tier table and po2 ceiling, on host and worker.
- `RISC0_METAL_RELEASE_SEG_BEFORE_REC`: the worker sets `1` when unset; `auto` is Z12's default and `0`
  is pre-Z12.
